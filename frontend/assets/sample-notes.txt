PAGE 1: WHAT MAKES A CELL?
Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living organisms.
A cell membrane regulates entry and exit of substances. It is selectively permeable: some substances cross more easily than others.
Prokaryotic cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus; their DNA is not enclosed by a nuclear membrane.
Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus.
Membrane selectivity helps regulate the cell's internal environment.

PAGE 2: ORGANELLES
In eukaryotic cells, the nucleus contains most cellular DNA.
Mitochondria are involved in aerobic cellular respiration and ATP production.
Ribosomes synthesize proteins. Cell structures divide the work of maintaining the cell.

PAGE 3: PLANT CELLS
A plant cell wall provides rigid support outside the cell membrane.
Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis in photosynthetic plant tissues.
A large central vacuole provides storage and supports turgor pressure.
The wall and vacuole contribute to plant-cell support.
